# Hero image brief — Chapter 19: Case: MEDA and the Outsider's Engineering Discipline

**Subject.** A precise diagnostic-style composition: a clinical pill bottle and a printed prescription strip rendered in flat schematic style sitting on a desk surface, overlaid with a subtle data-flow trace running underneath both objects — schema enforcement, deduplication, validation gate, publication. The pill bottle and prescription read as the *clinical domain*; the data-flow under them reads as the *engineering discipline*. The image argues the brand's claim — engineering rigor applied to clinical domain — by stacking the two visual languages on the same surface.

**Mood.** Clinical-precise, considered, slightly editorial. The composition should feel like a quietly authoritative documentary illustration rather than a marketing graphic. Boardroom-ready in the same register the brand argues.

**Negative space for title overlay.** Reserve the upper-right quadrant or full top band for chapter title and case-tagline overlay. Compose the still-life weighted to the lower-left so the title sits in the deep-navy upper expanse without visual competition.

**What the teaching image should make most visually prominent.** The *layered argument* — the clinical objects on top, the data-flow trace underneath, the two reading as one object rather than two. A single signal-red marker in one position (a small annotation point on the data trace, or a single pill in the bottle rendered in the brand's accent red) carries the *zero silent failures* commitment forward as a visible warning state.

**Palette.** Deep navy `#1A1A2E` ground, brand navy `#2C3E6B` for primary objects and primary line weight, signal red `#C0392B` exclusively for the single annotation accent, cloud blue `#EAF0FB` for highlights and surface card. Georgia or Cormorant for any small typography on the prescription strip; Courier New for the data-trace labels.

**Style references.** The diagrammatic restraint of *The New England Journal of Medicine* figure illustrations; the editorial register of *Wired* feature openers when they treat clinical subjects; the considered still-life work of Maciek Jasik's editorial photography. Avoid the saturated marketing register of generic AI-tool hero images and avoid stock-photo realism on the clinical objects.

**What to avoid.** No human hand or face. No literal stethoscope, white coat, or hospital iconography (the brand is engineering applied to clinical, not clinical practice). No "AI brain" sparkles. No GPT or OpenAI branding visible. No competing brand marks. No code on screen. The single red accent is the only saturated colour; everything else holds the navy / cloud-blue restraint.
