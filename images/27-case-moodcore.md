# Hero image brief — Chapter 27: Case: Moodcore — Sentiment Intelligence for Small Teams

**Subject.** A schematic composition rendering the multi-channel feedback ingestion as a converging set of vertical streams flowing toward a single contextual-sentiment output card. From the upper portion of the canvas, four labelled streams descend — *reviews, support tickets, surveys, social* — each rendered as a thin vertical band of small textual fragments at micro-type scale. The four streams converge into a single Moodcore-output card at the lower-centre showing a single nuanced sentiment annotation: *exhausted customer · about to churn · early signal* rather than a binary positive/negative tag.

**Mood.** Documentary-precise, slightly editorial. The composition should feel like an early-warning dashboard at the moment a quiet trend is being detected — not panicked, not flashy, just *we caught this earlier than the team would have caught it manually*. Restrained throughout; the orange accent appears once on the early-signal annotation.

**Negative space for title overlay.** Reserve the upper-left quadrant or full top band for chapter title and case-tagline overlay. The four converging streams begin near the top-right and move toward the lower-centre output card, leaving the upper-left in clean negative space for typography.

**What the teaching image should make most visually prominent.** The *single nuanced annotation on the output card* — *exhausted customer / about to churn / early signal* in orange — rather than a generic positive/negative tag in green/red. The visual argument is that *contextual nuance is the product*, and the output card carries that argument by showing the kind of annotation generic sentiment tools cannot produce.

**Palette.** Blue `#0000FF` for the primary streams and main line weight; Orange `#FFAA44` exclusively on the single early-signal annotation on the output card (the semantic-accent rule); Gray `#AAAAAA` for secondary text and inactive elements; near-white background. No additional saturated colors. Note: the deck commits to a basic palette at the minimum-viable stage; the image brief preserves that basic palette rather than upgrading it editorially, so the rendered hero matches the visual system at submission.

**Style references.** The dashboard register of *the Pudding* data-journalism illustrations; the considered restraint of editorial sentiment-analysis features in *Wired*; the schematic precision of Stripe's data-flow diagrams. Avoid the saturated marketing register of generic AI-tool hero graphics.

**What to avoid.** No literal Twitter, Yelp, Google Reviews, Zendesk, or SurveyMonkey logos. No "AI brain" iconography. No human face or hand. No emoji-style emotional iconography. No motion blur or speed lines. No additional saturated accents beyond the single orange early-signal annotation. The four converging streams plus the single nuanced output card are the entire composition.
