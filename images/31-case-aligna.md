# Hero image brief — Chapter 31: Case: Aligna — Brand Voice as a Decidable Question

**Subject.** A schematic composition rendering the three-state verdict architecture as the central visual argument: a single content-input card on the left flowing through a horizontal evaluation engine in the centre, terminating at three small parallel output verdict cards on the right — one labelled *PASS* with a subtle green check at micro-scale, one labelled *FLAG* with a small signal-cyan annotation marker, one labelled *REVIEW* with an AI-blue question-mark glyph. Each verdict card carries a thin confidence-score numeric annotation underneath in monospace. The composition argues that brand-voice evaluation is *three decidable states*, not a binary or a free-text suggestion.

**Mood.** Decision-infrastructure-precise, dark-mode considered, slightly editorial. The composition should feel like a quality-gate diagram from a thoughtful product-engineering reference document rather than a marketing graphic. Restraint everywhere; the three verdict glyphs are the only saturated detail, and even those are calibrated to the brand's restricted blue-cyan-and-quiet-green palette.

**Negative space for title overlay.** Reserve the upper-left quadrant or full top band for chapter title and case-tagline overlay. The input-card-to-verdict-cards composition sits at the lower two-thirds, weighted across the canvas at small-to-medium scale.

**What the teaching image should make most visually prominent.** The *three verdict cards rendered in parallel* — PASS, FLAG, REVIEW — each with its own confidence-score annotation underneath. The visual argument is that Aligna's category is created at the verdict-architecture level, not at the visual-flourish level. The three verdicts are the brand.

**Palette.** Deep Navy `#0F172A` ground; Midnight Black `#020617` for card surfaces; AI Blue `#2563EB` for primary line weight, the input-card border, and the REVIEW verdict glyph; Signal Cyan `#38BDF8` exclusively for the FLAG verdict marker (the semantic-accent rule). A muted green at lower saturation for the PASS verdict check (the deck does not specify a green hex; render as quiet, calibrated to the navy-and-blue palette rather than as bright). Sora for any display typography on the verdict labels; Inter for confidence-score annotations; JetBrains Mono if mono is used. No additional saturated colors.

**Style references.** The dark-mode decision-architecture register of GitHub's pull-request review interfaces; the considered restraint of Linear's roadmap views; the editorial precision of *MIT Technology Review* feature openers when treating decision-infrastructure topics. Avoid the saturated marketing register of generic AI-content-tooling hero graphics.

**What to avoid.** No literal Jasper, Writer, or Rytr brand marks. No Streamlit, Ollama, or n8n logos. No human figure or hand. No "AI brain" iconography. No literal *write/quill/pencil* imagery (the brand is decisively not in the writing category). No motion blur or speed lines. No additional saturated accents beyond the three verdict glyphs. The single input card, the evaluation engine, and the three parallel verdict cards are the entire composition.
