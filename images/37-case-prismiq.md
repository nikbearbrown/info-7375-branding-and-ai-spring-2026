# Hero image brief — Chapter 37: Case: Prismiq — The $4 Cable and the $1,500 Bill

**Subject.** A schematic composition rendering the *prism* metaphor at the brand-identity hero level. A single triangular prism shape sits at centre with raw-feedback lines (small textual fragments rendered as micro-type — *"slow service"*, *"loved it"*, *"never again"*, *"will return"*) entering from the upper-left edge as undifferentiated white light. The light passes through the prism and emerges on the lower-right edge as three separate calibrated outputs — *Sentiment · Severity · Response* — each in a slightly different but harmonized color band. Below the prism, a small annotated artifact-pair shows a single review card on the left with a small one-star indicator and the response-card on the right showing a personalized neighbor-voice reply.

**Mood.** Considered editorial, quietly authoritative, slightly literary — the *$4 Cable and $1,500 Bill* origin story carries a register that is closer to a small-business memoir than to SaaS marketing. The composition should feel like a book-jacket illustration for a business-craft volume rather than a tech-marketing graphic.

**Negative space for title overlay.** Reserve the upper third of the canvas for chapter title and case-tagline overlay. The prism sits at centre, the input fragments at upper-left, the three calibrated outputs at lower-right, and the review-and-response card pair anchors the lower edge.

**What the teaching image should make most visually prominent.** The *transformation through the prism* — undifferentiated input light becomes three calibrated output bands. The visual argument is that the brand's name and central metaphor are doing the same architectural work: refinement of raw data into actionable clarity. The single review-and-response artifact pair at the lower edge argues the response-automation pillar that separates the brand from sentiment-only competitors.

**Palette.** Calibrated to the deck's DM Serif Display + DM Sans editorial register: a warm-paper or near-cream backdrop; a deeply-saturated single accent for the prism itself (the deck's slide content does not commit to a specific named palette beyond the prism-triangle logomark and the editorial typography pair, so render with restraint — a single deep navy or charcoal for the prism's primary line weight; calibrated muted-color bands for the three output rays — sentiment in a quiet blue, severity in a quiet amber, response in a quiet green; near-black for typography). The prism is the only saturated detail.

**Style references.** The book-jacket-illustration register of literary-business titles (the considered restraint of *Atlantic* feature openers when treating small-business topics; the warm-paper aesthetic of *Cereal* magazine product features); the schematic precision of well-designed editorial diagrams in *Harvard Business Review*. Avoid the saturated marketing register of generic AI-tool hero graphics and avoid the cool-tech-blue aesthetic the cohort default leans on.

**What to avoid.** No literal pill bottles, electronics-shop imagery, or the $4 cable itself rendered photographically (the origin is a story, not a stock image). No human face or hand. No Claude, OpenAI, n8n, or Streamlit brand marks. No competing review-platform logos (Google, Yelp, Facebook). No saturated-marketing gradients or glow effects. No motion blur or speed lines. The prism, the input feedback fragments, the three calibrated output rays, and the single review-and-response artifact pair are the entire composition.
