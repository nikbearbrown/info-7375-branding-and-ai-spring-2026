## Worked Example

*Study this example before attempting Tier 1. After reading it, close it and try to recall the key steps from memory before moving on.*

**Problem:** A student has read the introduction and is asked: "What is this book trying to help you do that a Wikipedia article on branding could not?"

**Approach:**
Step 1: Note the introduction's distinction between *knowing the name of the subject* and *being able to use its ideas with judgment*.
Step 2: Identify the introduction's claim that the book teaches "a sequence of distinctions, practices, and recurring problems" — not a list of topics.
Step 3: Connect this to the concept of *transfer*: the book's goal is moving the reader from passive exposure to usable understanding — the ability to carry ideas from the page into a real problem or decision.

**Answer:** The book provides a structured path through the material — one that builds on itself chapter by chapter — so that the reader develops judgment, not just recognition. A Wikipedia article provides definitions; this book provides a route that turns those definitions into practical capacity.

**What to notice:** The introduction's central concept is *transfer*, not coverage. The book's success criterion is not "do you know the terms?" but "can you use the ideas when you encounter a real situation?"

**Ex 1**
*Model answer:* The book's central argument is that Branding and AI is best learned as "a sequence of distinctions, practices, and recurring problems" — not as a list of topics. This is contrasted with the way the subject is typically encountered: through terminology exposure and named examples without a connecting structure.
*Common error:* Students often paraphrase this as "the book teaches both theory and practice," which misses the structural claim — the sequence itself is what produces understanding, not just the presence of both dimensions.
*Chapter reference:* "The central argument" — opening section

---

**Ex 2**
*Answer:* False
*Correct because:* The introduction explicitly says the book "is not a substitute for practice, mentorship, experimentation, or domain-specific judgment."
*Common error:* Students who skim may infer that because the book is comprehensive, it is sufficient. The introduction directly disavows this.
*Chapter reference:* "What This Book Is Not"

---

**Ex 3**
*Model answer:* Transfer is the movement from reading an idea on the page to being able to use it in a real situation — a problem, a project, or a decision. It is what distinguishes a reader who understood the chapter from a reader who merely encountered it. The book's goal is not that students can recall the terms; it is that students can apply the ideas when they leave the course.
*What a strong answer includes:*
- The word "movement" or an equivalent — transfer implies change of location (from reading to action), not just storage
- The distinction between recognition and usable understanding
*Chapter reference:* "The Concept Running Through the Book"

---

**Ex 4**
*Model answer:* Reading as a book serves linear comprehension. Using as a reference serves lookup access. Integration into an intelligent textbook system (Medhavy) serves AI-assisted interaction. The introduction appears to prioritize linear comprehension — the opening paragraph describes the learner as needing "a route through the material," and the closing line ("This book is that path") frames the book as a traversal, not a database.
*Common error:* Students conflate the reference and AI-integration uses, or assume the AI-integration use is primary because the book mentions it in the AI section. The introduction's framing (a path, not a database) suggests the linear reading is primary.
*Chapter reference:* "What This Book Is" and "A Note About AI"

---

**Ex 5**
*Model answer:* The risk is stopping before transfer occurs. The introduction describes the book as a progression that builds practical judgment — the ability to use ideas with increasing sophistication. Stopping at Chapter 3 provides vocabulary without the cumulative understanding that later chapters build. The specific concept is *transfer*: the chapters work together to move the reader from knowing the names to being able to use the ideas in a real project. The student who stops at Chapter 3 has acquired exposure, not usable judgment.
*Common error:* Students often say the risk is "missing content" — which is correct but too generic. The stronger answer names what specifically will be missing: the compounding transfer that only happens across the full sequence.
*Chapter reference:* "The Concept Running Through the Book" and chapter summaries in "How This Book Is Organized"

---

**Ex 6**
*Where the error occurs:* The second sentence: "The book provides an exhaustive reference of all the important terms in the field and is designed to be read as a comprehensive dictionary of the subject."
*The misconception:* The student misunderstood the book's scope and design intention. The introduction explicitly says the book "does not try to say everything. It tries to say enough, in the right order" — the opposite of exhaustive coverage. The student conflated "structured introduction" with "comprehensive reference."
*Corrected version:* "The book is a structured introduction that teaches vocabulary in the context of the distinctions and practices that connect it — not a comprehensive dictionary, but a path through the material that builds practical judgment."
*Chapter reference:* "What This Book Is" and "What This Book Is Not"

---

**Ex 7**
*Model answer:* The strongest point in the AI response is recognizing that AI tools are relevant to the book's subject matter. The significant error is treating AI as "the primary subject of study" — something students learn to build. The introduction says AI is "infrastructure for practical AI-assisted authorship, analysis, and production" — meaning AI tools are used *in the learning process and book system*, not that building AI is the course objective. The corrected sentence: "The book treats AI tools as infrastructure that supports learning — making definitions searchable, examples interactive, and practice sequences adaptive — while the primary subject remains branding strategy and the creative engineering mindset." To verify: read the book's chapter list; the chapters cover frameworks (archetypes, PRDs, visual identity) that predate AI, confirming that AI is a delivery and augmentation mechanism, not the primary subject.
*What a strong answer includes:*
- Distinguishing AI as infrastructure for learning vs. AI as course content
- The specific term "infrastructure" defined in context
- A verification step that tests the claim using the chapter list, not just re-reading the sentence
*Chapter reference:* "A Note About AI"

---

**Ex 8**
*Model answer:* Comprehensive coverage would provide more material but would not ensure the reader can use it — exposure without structure rarely produces transfer. Sequencing and selectivity work together: sequencing builds each concept on the last so understanding accumulates, and selectivity ensures that the reader encounters only the ideas that move understanding forward at each stage. Transfer requires that the reader carry an idea from reading into practice, and that movement is easier when the path is clear and the steps are manageable rather than when everything is presented simultaneously.
*Common error:* Students describe selectivity as simply "not overwhelming the reader" — which captures one benefit but misses the transfer argument: the omissions are not just comfort, they are strategic constraints that keep the path toward usable understanding clear.
*Chapter reference:* "What This Book Is Not" and "The Concept Running Through the Book"
