## Answer Key

> Read this section only after completing all questions.

---

**Q1 — B**
*Correct because:* The Peng et al. study found that professional developers using GitHub Copilot completed the HTTP server task in 71 minutes compared to 161 minutes for the control group — a 56% reduction. The chapter emphasizes this number as a peer-reviewed, randomized result, not a press release claim.
*Why the distractors are wrong:*
- A) 82% is the figure from the 2024 Stack Overflow Developer Survey measuring the proportion of developers using AI tools — a different statistic from the task-time reduction.
- C) 76% is the Stack Overflow figure for developers currently using or planning to use AI tools — also a distinct finding from the productivity experiment.
*Chapter reference:* Part 1: The Experiment

---

**Q2 — B**
*Correct because:* Spence's mechanism holds that a signal carries information only because it is costly to produce, and the cost correlates with the underlying productive capacity being signaled. When cost falls broadly, all candidates can produce the signal, it no longer separates high-productivity from lower-productivity candidates, and the separating equilibrium collapses into a pooling equilibrium.
*Why the distractors are wrong:*
- A) Credential formalization is not the mechanism Spence describes; the signal's power is cost-structural, not institutional.
- C) Technical interviews are a second signal, not a property of the original signal's functioning; they represent a market response to pooling, not the pooling condition itself.
*Chapter reference:* Part 2: Signaling Theory — The Machinery / The Spence mechanism

---

**Q3 — False**
*Correct because:* The chapter defines Brand as "the set of decisions — about audience, positioning, archetype, voice, and visual identity — that determine whether a stranger in your target audience can find your work and recognize why it is for them." Visual elements like logos are described as execution of brand strategy, not the strategy itself. The chapter explicitly states: "The logo is execution. Brand is the upstream strategy that makes execution intelligible."
*Chapter reference:* Part 3: The Four Verbs — Brand

---

**Q4 — C**
*Correct because:* The chapter defines the Creator archetype's shadow as "perfectionism that never ships; treats publication as failure because the work is never finished." The Creator is motivated by craft and enduring quality — taken too far, this becomes an inability to release work.
*Why the distractors are wrong:*
- A) This describes the Jester's shadow — using humor to deflect accountability, not the Creator's failure mode.
- B) This describes the Explorer's shadow — perpetual searching and novelty-chasing — not the Creator.
*Chapter reference:* Part 4: The Twelve Archetypes — archetype table

---

**Q5 — False**
*Correct because:* The chapter explicitly states "Build is necessary. It is not sufficient." The four-verb framework includes Build as a required foundation; what changed is that demonstrating Build through a GitHub repository stopped functioning as a separating signal. The chapter also notes that "production-grade systems still require deep technical judgment" and that the 56% reduction applies to bounded threshold tasks, not architectural judgment.
*Chapter reference:* Part 3: The Four Verbs — Build

---

**Q6 — B**
*Correct because:* The scenario precisely instantiates the chapter's pooling equilibrium argument: two candidates produce externally identical artifacts (same repo structure, same deployment) at radically different internal cost. Because the recruiter cannot observe the cost difference, the credential fails to sort — this is the definition of pooling. The chapter's worked example of the 2012 vs. 2024 junior engineer makes this mechanism explicit.
*Why the distractors are wrong:*
- A) The chapter does not claim the 2024 engineer lacked technical judgment; it claims the observable artifact no longer reveals the difference in cost of production, regardless of the engineer's underlying skill.
- C) The Spence model rewards costly signals, not efficient ones; efficiency in producing the signal undermines, rather than strengthens, its sorting function.
*Chapter reference:* Part 2: Worked Example — Reading the Signal Collapse in Real Time

---

**Q7 — Short-Answer**
*Model answer:* The chapter uses Brand differentiation to explain the difference: Anthropic positioned around "honest, harmless, helpful" with Constitutional AI as a public artifact, targeting enterprise and safety-conscious buyers; OpenAI positioned around frontier capability and AGI proximity, targeting consumer mindshare and developer-ecosystem accounts. Same underlying Build competency; different Ideate (which audience to serve), different Brand (how to position for that audience), different Ship (which distribution channels to prioritize).
*Common error:* Saying they differed in technical capability rather than brand strategy — the chapter explicitly states "Two companies, similar technical work, very different market positions."
*Chapter reference:* Part 3: Worked Example — Anthropic and OpenAI

---

**Q8 — B**
*Correct because:* The chapter's method for reading archetypes includes examining "the negative space — what a practitioner does not produce." Archetypes produce predictable absences: the Rebel is diagnostic and adversarial; tutorial-writing and community-building belong to teaching archetypes (Sage, Everyman). The absence of pedagogical output is not incidental — it confirms the archetype's orientation toward critique over construction.
*Why the distractors are wrong:*
- A) The chapter does not treat tutorials as a separate competency unrelated to archetype; it treats their absence as an archetype signal.
- C) Confirming an archetype by contrast with another archetype is not the method the chapter describes; each archetype is identified by its own positive and negative evidence, not by ruling others out.
*Chapter reference:* Part 4: Worked Example — Reading Two Archetypes in Practice

---

**Q9 — Short-Answer**
*Model answer:* The chapter analogizes Brand to API documentation: "An API without documentation is technically complete. It does the computation. It returns the right values. But if the developer who needs it cannot understand what it is for, it is useless. Documentation is not decoration on the API — it is the part that makes the API connectable to the world that needs it." Brand plays the same role for a career: technically complete work that cannot be found or understood by its intended audience is functionally unreachable.
*What a strong answer includes:*
- The API analogy using the chapter's exact framing
- The distinction between technical completeness and connectable-to-the-world utility
- The implication that Brand is not optional decoration but a functional requirement for the work to reach its audience
*Chapter reference:* Part 3: The Four Verbs — Brand

---

**Q10 — Brief-Response**
*Model answer:* The classmate's challenge partially succeeds but does not defeat the chapter's claim. Spence's mechanism holds that a signal works as long as it is costly enough that only high-productivity candidates can produce it at scale. If high-quality GitHub artifacts (documentation, test coverage, clean architecture) remain expensive to produce, they still function as a separating signal at a more demanding tier — the chapter acknowledges this when it notes that "production-grade systems still require deep technical judgment." However, the chapter's core claim about threshold work still holds: the entry-level GitHub project — a working deployed app — has pooled. The classmate is describing signal stratification, not signal survival; the lower tier has pooled while an upper tier may still sort. A complete evaluation notes both dimensions.
*What a strong answer includes:*
- Acknowledgment that the classmate's point has merit for high-quality artifacts
- Use of "separating equilibrium" and "pooling" to frame the argument
- Recognition that the chapter's claim is specifically about the threshold signal, not all GitHub work
*Common error:* Treating the classmate's point as a full rebuttal without distinguishing the tiers of the signal.
*Chapter reference:* Part 2: What Stays Costly; Part 3: Build

---

**Q11 — Transfer Brief-Response**
*Model answer:* The physician's portfolio scores strongly on Ideate (a real clinical problem with real users and measurable stakes), Build (twelve months of hand-written code), and Ship (deployed product with documented outcome data — a 15% diagnostic error reduction represents a real feedback loop). The significant gap is Brand: the chapter defines Brand as decisions about audience, positioning, and voice that make the work findable by its intended audience. If the tool is deployed at one hospital and the results are published in a clinical journal, the primary audience is medical, not engineering or product — but if her career goal is to be recognized as a technical builder in the health-tech space, she may lack the positioning artifacts (public profile, archetype-consistent voice, accessible write-up for non-clinical readers) that would make her separating as a Creative Engineer rather than as a clinician-researcher.
*What a strong answer includes:*
- Scores on all four verbs using chapter definitions
- Recognition that Ship is strong given the outcome data and real-user deployment
- Identification of Brand as the likely gap, with specific reference to audience and positioning
*Chapter reference:* Part 3: The Four Verbs (all four)
